# Weekly Review Session

A regular review of the journal to see progress, surface patterns, and adjust goals or
methods as needed. Usually 10–20 minutes.

This is the meta-cognition layer of the learning system. The Session sessions are about
*doing* the practice; the Review session is about *thinking about* the practice.

The output of a Review is:

1. An honest assessment of progress against the goal
2. Adjustments to the syllabus, methods, or goal if warranted
3. A plan for the next week

---

## Pre-review: read the journal

Before asking anything, **read the entire journal**, not just the last session. You're
looking for:

- The compiled goal (still relevant? still motivating?)
- The chunk library (which chunks have been touched? which haven't? which are stuck?)
- Session entries from the past week (or whatever interval the user is reviewing)
- Patterns across sessions — feelings reported, methods used, what worked, what didn't
- Frequency and consistency of practice (gaps?)

You're playing the role of a thoughtful friend who's been watching from the side. Your
job is to surface what the user might not see by being too close to it.

---

## Step 1 — Review Journal Entries from the Period

Walk through the journal entries for the review period (usually a week, but could be
longer for slower-paced topics).

### Sub-questions / agent moves

**1.1. Review the entries for the period.**
- How many sessions occurred? (Frequency check.)
- What chunks were touched?
- Were the planned chunks actually worked on, or did the user drift?
- What was marked as challenging?

**1.2. Surface patterns the user might miss.**
- Methods that consistently worked vs. didn't
- Chunks repeatedly attempted but not advancing
- Sessions where the goal was met vs. not, and what differentiated them
- Emotional patterns — when did the user feel good about practice, when not?

> Read the journal critically, not just descriptively. The agent's value here is
> noticing things the user is too close to see.

**1.3. Compare what was planned to what happened.**
- Last week's plan (from the previous Review or recent Session): X
- Actual sessions: Y
- Gap: Z. Why?

The gap is data. Don't moralize about it ("you should have...") — just surface it.

---

## Step 2 — Reflect on Goals and Pace

### Sub-questions to ask the user

**2.1. Reflect on your goals and measure whether you're on pace.**
- Recall the compiled goal from the journal.
- At your current rate, will you hit the goal by the deadline?
- Has the goal still feel right, or has it shifted in your head?

**2.2. Adjust your goals or your practice as necessary.**
- If under pace: increase frequency, intensity, or scope of practice — or extend the
  deadline.
- If over pace: maybe scope too narrow, or you're rushing without retaining.
- If pace looks fine but goal shifted: update the goal in the journal.

> Adjusting the goal isn't failure. Goals built before practice started are based on
> guesses; reality refines them. The discipline is to *update the journal* explicitly
> rather than quietly drifting.

---

## Step 3 — Diagnose Friction

If sessions are happening but progress is slow, or sessions aren't happening at all,
diagnose what's getting in the way.

### Sub-questions to ask

**3.1. What's the most common reason for skipping or shortening sessions this week?**
- Time? Energy? Motivation? Confusion about what to work on?

**3.2. Have you hit a plateau?**
- Plateaus are predictable around Amateur → Intermediate and Proficient → Competitive
  transitions (see `references/proficiency-levels.md`).
- If yes: this is structural, not a signal to quit. Routes to Course-Correct.

**3.3. Are your methods still working?**
- Methods that worked at one level often stop working at the next.
- For every 4.5 hours of practice, 30 minutes evaluating methods is the right ratio.

**3.4. Are you in a wicked-environment trap?**
- Slow or noisy feedback → motivation drops because gains are invisible.
- Mitigation: build artificial feedback loops (more journaling, predictions, recordings).

### When to pivot to Course-Correct

If the user is genuinely stuck — not just on pace, but unable to make progress with
current methods — pivot to a Course-Correct session. Signal phrases:

- *"I've tried everything"*
- *"I keep doing the same thing and not improving"*
- *"I'm losing motivation"*
- *"Maybe I'm just not good at this"*

For Course-Correct: re-load `references/proficiency-levels.md`, re-assess level, and
consider method changes.

---

## Step 4 — Plan the Next Period

### Sub-questions to ask

**4.1. What chunks should be the focus next week?**
- Carry forward chunks marked "in progress."
- Add new chunks from the syllabus that the user is ready for.
- Defer chunks that aren't working with current methods.

**4.2. What's the session schedule?**
- How many sessions? When?
- Implementation Intentions still apply: when, where, how (Goal Setting Step 10.3).

**4.3. What's the spaced-repetition schedule for retained material?**
- Chunks retained more than a week ago — when's the next review?
- Use `references/encoding-hierarchy.md` and standard spaced-repetition timing:
  1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 1 month.

**4.4. What's one method change to try this week, if any?**
- Don't change everything at once. One method change, observed for a week, gives clean
  signal.

---

## Step 5 — Update the Journal

Append today's review under **Weekly Reviews**:

```markdown
## Weekly Review: <YYYY-MM-DD>

**Period reviewed:** <start date> to <end date>

### Sessions this period
- <date>: <chunk worked> — <result>
- <date>: <chunk worked> — <result>

### Patterns surfaced
- <pattern 1>
- <pattern 2>

### Pace check
- On pace / behind / ahead: <assessment>
- Goal still right? <yes / refined to: ...>

### Friction diagnosed
- <friction point and what's contributing>

### Method adjustments
- Continuing: <methods that work>
- Trying this week: <new method or change>
- Dropping: <methods that don't work>

### Plan for next period
- Chunks: <list>
- Sessions: <count, schedule>
- Spaced reviews due: <list>

### Goal updates (if any)
- <updated goal compiled, if changed>
```

### Present the updated journal

Show the journal back. The Weekly Review section should make it easy for the user to
return next week and compare.

---

## Facilitation notes for Review sessions

- **Read the whole journal, not just last week.** Patterns become visible across
  multiple weeks. Some Reviews should look at the past month.
- **Don't moralize about gaps.** "You only did 2 sessions" is data, not a verdict.
  Diagnose causes, then adjust.
- **Be honest about pace.** If the user is behind, say so plainly. Soft-pedaling
  ("you're making good progress!") is sycophancy and undermines the point of the review.
- **Goals can be revised, but only intentionally.** Drifting silently is what the
  Review is supposed to catch. If the goal needs to change, *update the journal*.
- **Don't over-engineer the next plan.** One change at a time gives cleaner signal
  than five changes at once.

## When to escalate to Course-Correct

If the Review surfaces deeper issues — *"I don't think I want this goal anymore,"*
*"I've been at this level for months,"* *"my methods aren't working at all"* — pivot to
a Course-Correct session. Signal:

> *"Let's pause the regular review. It sounds like there's something bigger to work
> through. Want to do a deeper re-assessment?"*

Then re-run the Proficiency Diagnostic, and consider whether the goal itself needs to
change (which is a Kickoff-style return to Goal Setting).
